PLEASE REMAIN SEATED
====================

A game about being the only person on an aeroplane who has correctly assessed the situation.

The pitch
---------

Transnational Flight 447, cruising. Something in the overhead bin above seat 14C is on fire. You
are the only passenger who thinks this is worth discussing. The plane begins its descent in
fifteen minutes. The cabin crew would like you to return to your seat.

You cannot put the fire out. This is not a difficulty setting, it is the premise: the seat of the
fire is a lithium cell in a vape pen inside a hard-shell suitcase inside a closed bin, and it
reignites. Everything you do to it buys minutes, never a win. What you can do is carry people
forward, one at a time, while the cabin explains to you that you are overreacting.

The clock only moves when you do
--------------------------------

Time is the resource and the only resource. Nothing happens while you think. Every action has a
price in seconds and the fire, the smoke and the passengers advance exactly that many seconds when
you pay it. Pouring a bottle of water on the fire is nine seconds. Carrying an adult twelve rows
forward is fifty-one. Arguing with a flight attendant about whether you are allowed to be standing
is forty seconds and accomplishes nothing, which is the joke and also the lesson.

Nine hundred seconds. Sixty-one people. Do the arithmetic and despair; that is the design.

The five systems
----------------

**The cabin** is a grid, nose left and tail right: twenty-eight columns of rows, nine rows of
seats and aisle (A B C, aisle, D E F, and the two window walls). Galleys fore and aft, two
lavatories, four exits, a cockpit door that stays shut.

**The fire** is per-tile intensity, fuel and oxygen. It creeps along seat cushions, races through
the overhead bins, and stalls in the aisle. Suppression drops intensity; it does not remove the
seat of the fire, which sits at a floor value and climbs back. Foam, halon, water, ice, coffee and
seven kinds of duty-free spirit all interact with it differently and one of them is a bad idea.

**The smoke** is the thing that actually kills, and the game is honest about that. It fills from
the ceiling down, moves four times faster than the fire, and does not care about the fire's
intensity once it exists. Passengers below the smoke line are fine. Passengers in it are not.

**The passengers** are sixty-one named people with a weight, a temperament, a seat and an opinion.
Most of them will not move. Some of them will move if the right person asks. A handful of them,
correctly persuaded, become **helpers** who carry other passengers - and this, not the fire and not
your own two arms, is the only route to a genuinely good score. The game will never tell you this.

**The crew** run their procedure. The procedure is good. The procedure is for a galley oven fire.

Credibility
-----------

You begin at zero credibility and everyone is right not to believe you: you are a passenger out of
your seat during the meal service, pointing at a closed bin. Credibility rises when the evidence
becomes public - visible flame, the smoke detector, a burn on your hand, the purser's own eyes -
and every social action is gated on it. At high credibility the crew stop obstructing you and
start helping. Reaching that point takes about four minutes you do not have.

Characters
----------

Ten, each a different reading of the same fifteen minutes. A retired fire officer who is slow. An
influencer who is fast and filming. A strongman who can carry two. A nun the cabin actually
listens to. An aviation lawyer who wins arguments. A vet who can revive the unconscious. A nervous
flyer who gets better as things get worse. A deadheading captain who can shorten the flight, which
is not obviously good. An eight-year-old travelling alone who fits places adults do not. An air
marshal with a firearm and no useful application for it.

Five stats - **strength, speed, lungs, nerve, voice** - plus one signature ability and one flaw.

The loadout
-----------

Before boarding you pick items out of a bag, to a weight limit. Thirty-odd of them, and the
comedy is that the correct answer is boring: water, a blanket, a wet towel. The interesting
answer is duty-free perfume, an emotional support iguana and a harmonica.

Hundreds of actions
-------------------

The action list is the game. Roughly three hundred hand-written actions across seven decks - fire,
people, crew, cabin, self, items, and the desperate ones - each with its own cost, its own
conditions and its own line of text, and the people deck multiplies by whoever is next to you. The
list is filtered to what is actually possible where you are standing, so the screen offers you a
dozen at a time and the other two hundred and eighty are the game's memory of everything you did
not think of.

The ending
----------

The plane lands. It always lands. Then the game writes you an incident report in the flat voice of
an air accident investigator, which is the only voice in which any of this is funny: souls on
board, souls accounted for, the actions you took in the order you took them, and one paragraph of
findings about the passenger in 21F who spent the descent asking you to sit down.

Twelve endings. None of them are "you put the fire out."
